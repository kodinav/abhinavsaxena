Almost nothing you know arrived directly. It came through a chain of instruments and people, and now, increasingly, through models. Social epistemology has studied human chains for decades; the machine links are new.

In this experiment a fact about the world passes through five links. Each has a reliability and a cost of verification. You decide, for each, whether to check or to trust. At the end you learn whether you *know* the claim — and, if the chain failed, where responsibility for the failure falls under different accounts.

**What to notice.** Trusting everything is cheap and usually works, which is exactly what makes it dangerous. Verifying everything is impossible. The interesting positions are in between — and they distribute responsibility very differently depending on whether the unreliable link was a person or a model.
