Turing's 1950 proposal was a test of *behaviour*: if an interrogator cannot tell the machine from the person, the question "can machines think?" is to be replaced by "can machines pass?" Seventy years on, systems pass with ease, and the question has not gone away. Something in the test was measuring the judge.

This experiment makes that explicit. You are the judge. Decide how much each kind of evidence — fluent language, flexible behaviour, a body, a history, a nervous system, the ability to suffer — should count. The instrument then scores five candidates according to *your* criteria.

**What to notice.** No weighting produces a ranking everyone accepts. Weight language and the model outscores the octopus. Weight the body and the corporation vanishes. The verdict is downstream of a theory of mind you may not know you hold.
