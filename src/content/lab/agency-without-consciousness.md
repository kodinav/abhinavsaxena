Dennett proposed that we attribute beliefs and desires to a system whenever doing so lets us predict it — the *intentional stance*. Critics reply that prediction is not the point: to act is to act *for* reasons, and reasons need a subject.

In this experiment you watch a small population of agents seek resources and avoid hazards. You can add or remove capacities. With each change, four accounts of agency — behavioural, intentional-stance, reasons-responsive and phenomenal — deliver their verdicts.

**What to notice.** The behavioural and intentional-stance accounts say yes long before the others. The phenomenal account never says yes, because nothing you can switch on in the simulation is experience. Whether that is a limit of the simulation or a limit of the account is the question.
