Almost everything any of us knows, we know because someone told us. Social epistemology takes that fact seriously: it studies testimony, trust, expertise, disagreement and the institutions through which knowledge is produced and distributed.

Artificial systems are now part of those institutions. My work asks how the classical questions change when one of the "speakers" in an epistemic community is a model: whether its outputs count as testimony, what it would mean to trust it, and how responsibility for belief is distributed along a chain that runs from data through model through interface to a person.

I am especially concerned with **epistemic injustice** in this setting. Systems trained on the historical distribution of who has been believed can inherit and automate that distribution — and, unlike a prejudiced hearer, they cannot be confronted.
