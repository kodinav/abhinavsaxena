Much of the ethics of AI is written as if the philosophical work were done and only the application remained. I do not think that is true. The core concepts — responsibility, explanation, fairness, trust — were developed for human agents acting in human institutions, and each of them strains when the agent is a system that learns, acts at scale and cannot be asked why.

My approach is to take those strains as data. The **responsibility gap** is not a hole into which accountability disappears; it is a map of how control and knowledge are actually distributed in sociotechnical systems, and reading the map carefully usually shows that responsibility has relocated rather than vanished.

I am also interested in the demand for explanation. There is a difference between an explanation that is causally accurate and one that would satisfy the person affected by a decision. Much "explainable AI" pursues the first while the moral demand is for the second.
