The ethics and philosophy of technology study what technologies are, how they change what people can know and do, and how they should be judged. Philosophy of technology is one of my areas of competence, and both "Beyond Consent" and "Assertion Without a Speaker" are, in part, arguments in it: one about what an architecture does to the people it decides about, the other about what kind of instrument a language model is.

A widow's papers are lost, but the clerk who has paid her pension for a decade knows her and pays it anyway. In a discretionary bureaucracy the criterion of entitlement is evidence, weighed by someone present who can recognise the manifestly entitled when the criterion fails. A biometrically gated, database-linked system has no such person. The fingerprint reads or it does not.

## Architecture, not technique

In ["Beyond Consent"](/publications/beyond-consent/) I scope the argument by architecture rather than technique. An [algorithmic welfare system](/glossary/algorithmic-welfare/) combines automated decisioning, a criterion enforced without discretion at the point of application, and integration across databases. Whether the system learns is beside the point. Aadhaar's authentication layer is biometric matching and database linkage, not machine learning in the contemporary sense, and the argument governs it all the same. The GDPR and the EU AI Act draw their own scope the same way, covering automated processing broadly rather than machine learning narrowly.

What the architecture removes is not speed or reach but a standpoint: the position, inside the act of recognition, from which a person's evident standing could correct the system's verdict. Lipsky showed that front-line discretion is where policy is actually made; Bovens and Zouridis traced the move from street-level to system-level bureaucracy, in which decision criteria are fixed in software in advance. I add that, for some systems, this enforcement does something prior: it produces the standing of the people it decides about.

The claim is structural, not sentimental. Discretion is also the medium of prejudice and petty domination, and automation was partly a response to that. But once discretion is gone the institution must rebuild a standpoint of contestation outside the transaction, in a form the people it fails can actually reach. Sending someone whose fingerprints have failed to an online grievance portal does not do that. Scale and speed make the loss more consequential; they are not its source.

## Instruments we cannot see inside

The other strand concerns instruments. The classical epistemology of instruments assumes transparent calibration: for a thermometer we know what it is calibrated to, by what procedure, and how it fails. Philosophers of science have studied the loss of that transparency under the name [epistemic opacity](/glossary/epistemic-opacity/), following Humphreys, and computational reliabilists such as Durán and Formanek have shown how reliance on opaque processes can still be warranted through validation, robustness analysis and a track record. I count them allies.

In ["Assertion Without a Speaker"](/publications/assertion-without-a-speaker/) I argue that a language model is opaque in that ordinary sense and degenerate as well. A barometer is opaque to a user who does not understand aneroid cells, but it is still connected to the pressure, and she can learn how or ask someone who knows. With a language model what is missing is not the user's access to the calibration but the calibration: nothing in the production of an open-domain answer is keyed to the truth of the particular content emitted. That is why [instrument reading](/glossary/instrument-reading/) puts the load on the user, and why, when such an instrument misleads, the remedies are engineering ones rather than social ones. I take this up in [instrument or informant?](/questions/instrument-or-informant/)

## What I have not settled

Whether removing discretion makes a difference in kind or only in degree is the hinge of the first strand, and a reader who thinks contestation is never fully internal even to human bureaucracies would scope my claim more narrowly. Aadhaar does provide exception channels, manual overrides and grievance procedures; whether any of them restores the standpoint, rather than deferring its loss, depends on whether it is available, reliable and exercised at the moment of the transaction. On the second, the open question is what validation can establish about an instrument for which there is, as yet, no general method of predicting reliability at the level of a single output. A further paper, "Consignation Without Custody", is under review.
