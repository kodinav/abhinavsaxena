The existentialist tradition — Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, Heidegger, Sartre, Beauvoir — treated the self not as a thing one has but as a task one undertakes, under conditions one did not choose. I find this vocabulary unusually useful for thinking about technologies designed to anticipate, recommend and decide on our behalf.

The question is not whether such systems are useful (they obviously are) but what they do to the *task*: whether a choice that has been made easy is still, in the relevant sense, mine. I take this to be a question about freedom in Beauvoir's sense — freedom as something exercised under and against conditions, not in their absence.
