Phenomenology — the tradition running from Husserl through Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty — is often treated as the opposite of the philosophy of AI: humanist, anti-computational, concerned with lived experience rather than information. I think that framing is a mistake. Phenomenology has the most detailed available accounts of intentionality, of embodiment and of how tools become transparent in use, and each of these is directly relevant to systems that produce meaning-like output without a body or a world.

I use phenomenological method as a discipline of description: before asking whether a system understands, describe carefully what it is like to be answered by it.
