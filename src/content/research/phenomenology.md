Phenomenology is the philosophical study of experience as it is lived: how things show up for someone, described carefully before any theory of what they are. The tradition runs from Husserl through Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty. It is a wider interest of mine, not an area in which I have published.

It is often cast as the opposite of the philosophy of AI, humanist and anti-computational, concerned with lived experience rather than information. I read it differently. It has some of the most careful accounts we have of intentionality, of embodiment, and of how tools withdraw from attention when they work, as Heidegger's hammer does in use and the blind man's stick does in Merleau-Ponty. Each bears on systems that take part in conversation.

## How an exchange feels and what it is

Being answered by a chatbot can feel very like being told. The answer arrives in sentences, addressed to you, fluent and to the point. Freiman builds this into his concept of [AI testimony](/glossary/ai-testimony/), one of whose conditions is that the output be phenomenologically similar to human speech. In ["Assertion Without a Speaker"](/publications/assertion-without-a-speaker/) I reply that conditions of this kind classify outputs without saying whether warrant passes through them. How an exchange feels does not settle what it is.

That reply is not a dismissal of phenomenology; it is the reason the description matters. The structure of the exchange, on my account, is that of reading an instrument, while its felt character is that of being told. A hearer who goes by the felt character will defer where she should calibrate. A careful description of how an answer presents itself, and of what in it invites trust, seems to me part of understanding why that happens.

## What I take from it

What draws me to the tradition is its discipline: before asking whether a system understands, describe what it is like to be answered by it. I take no position here on whether intentionality requires a lived body or a horizon of concern, and my arguments about testimony are built so as not to need one. These are questions I read in, not ones I claim to have answered.
