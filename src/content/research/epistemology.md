The classical analysis of knowledge assumed a knower: a subject who believes, who has reasons, who can be asked to defend them. Machine epistemology puts pressure on each of those assumptions in turn.

My work here is mainly on **justification**. Internalists locate it in reasons the believer can access; externalists locate it in the reliability of the process that produced the belief. A model's output is a strange case for both: the process may be highly reliable, yet no one can access the reasons — the developer knows the training procedure, the user knows the answer, and no one knows why this answer.

I am also interested in what I call the **epistemology of the interface**: how the design of the surface through which we receive information — a search result, a chat window, a confidence score — shapes what we take ourselves to know.
