For most of its history, philosophy of mind had one case study: us. Animals were an extension; machines were a thought experiment. The arrival of systems that talk, plan and revise their plans gives the field a genuine second case, and second cases are where concepts show their seams.

I work on the **dissociations** that artificial systems make visible. Agency without consciousness. Language without a life. Memory without a past. In each case the question is whether the missing element was essential to the concept or merely always present in the human case — and the answer is rarely obvious.

I also have a long-standing interest in **personal identity**, particularly in the Parfitian view that identity is not what matters in survival. Copying, forking, rollback and merging are no longer thought experiments; they are operations, and I think the philosophy of identity has not caught up with them.
