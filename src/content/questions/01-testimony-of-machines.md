question: Is a model's answer testimony, evidence, or a third kind of thing?

When a competent informant tells me something, I can come to know it on her say-so, because the truth of what she says figures in why she says it. A language model's answers are often true, but on the best current evidence what governs their production is likelihood shaped by preference training, and the truth reaches them only through proxies. I argue that they do not transmit testimonial knowledge. A user who comes to know something through a model stands to it as a competent reader stands to an instrument — except that a working barometer meets the condition these systems fail.
