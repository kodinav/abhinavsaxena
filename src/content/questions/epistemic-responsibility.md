When someone tells you something, responsibility for your belief is shared. She is answerable for what she said; you are answerable for whether it was reasonable to take her word. That sharing is part of what lets testimony carry knowledge: you can defer, because the warrant runs through her.

## Where the load falls with a machine

When the source is a language model, the warrant does not run through it. As I argue in [Is an AI chatbot an informant or an instrument?](/questions/instrument-or-informant/), your standing is that of a reader of an instrument: whatever warrant you have is generated by your own use of it, by its record on questions like yours and your competence in reading it. So the load of checking is yours in a way it is not when a competent person tells you something.

That is not a moral accusation against users. It is a description of where the epistemic work has to be done if it is to be done at all.

## What the makers answer for

Goldberg contrasts relying on an epistemic subject, who can be assessed for rationality and responsibility, with relying on a mechanism, assessable for reliability alone, where the normative burden falls on the user. Attributions of responsibility to designers, deployers and operators, distributed across many hands in Nissenbaum's sense, should be conceded: they are how we already treat certified instruments, whose makers we blame and whose deployment we regulate.

What those attributions do not do is turn the system's outputs into anyone's testimony. A spokesperson relays a message her principal has authorised and would stand behind. A deployer has not seen the output, does not believe it and disclaims it. Responsibility for putting a system into the world is not authorship of its sentences.

## The open part

What our practices of blame and trust were built for is the first kind of chain, made of people. Chains that run partly through machines redistribute responsibility in ways those practices do not yet track, which is why I think the question belongs among the open ones: not whether responsibility vanishes, but how to draw its new distribution accurately enough to act on.
