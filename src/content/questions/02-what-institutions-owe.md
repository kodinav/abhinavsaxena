---
question: What does an institution owe the people whose standing it produces?
order: 2
emphasis: ["owe", "standing it produces"]
areas: [ethics-of-ai, political-philosophy]
publication: beyond-consent
concepts: [ethics, responsibility, autonomy]
---

Data protection and AI governance turn on consent: processing is legitimate if the person has authorised it. But some systems do not act on people whose standing precedes them; they produce it. The labourer whose worn fingerprints fail at the ration shop is not withholding consent — she is failing to register as someone who could give it. Where an institution produces its subjects' operative standing, I argue, its legitimacy rests not on their authorisation but on obligations they cannot waive: to make them legible, not to abandon them, and to remedy failure through channels that do not presuppose the legibility that failed.
