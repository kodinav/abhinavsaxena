seoTitle: What Algorithmic Welfare Systems Owe
---

The critique of consent in algorithmic welfare is the diagnosis; the destination is a positive account of what such institutions owe. If a system produces the operative standing of the people it governs, legitimacy cannot be traced back to their authorisation. It has to be located in the institution's obligations. I call the resulting principle the Constitutive Obligation Principle (COP).

> **The Constitutive Obligation Principle.** Where an institution produces its subjects' operative standing as entitlement-bearers, its legitimacy depends not on their authorisation but on discharging non-waivable obligations of legibility, non-abandonment and remediation.

Each obligation answers to one of the three structural features of [constitutive dependence](/questions/what-is-constitutive-dependence/), rather than being added from outside.

## Legibility

Because the institution produces recognition, it cannot treat its criteria of recognition as natural facts beyond its responsibility. The obligation of legibility requires that it make its subjects legible under conditions responsive to the material reality of their lives. A fingerprint criterion that systematically fails the hands of people who do manual labour is not a neutral test that some people happen to fail. It is a criterion the institution chose, and it fails the people the welfare system exists to protect.

## Non-abandonment

Because the institution can revise its subjects' recognition through its own operations, it must not withdraw what it produced without meeting specific burdens of justification. Once it has constituted someone as an operative entitlement-bearer, a cancelled card, a failed link between databases or a lapsed authentication is not a clerical event. It is a withdrawal of recognition, and it needs the justification that withdrawing recognition requires.

## Remediation

Because the subject has no standpoint outside the institution from which to refuse or contest, she must have a substantive, not merely formal, pathway within it when recognition fails. An institution that answers fingerprint failure by directing the person to an online grievance portal discharges no genuine obligation; it redoubles the failure. The pathway has to reach the person the system cannot currently see.

## Why recognition theory

The view locates algorithmic misrecognition within the tradition of Honneth, Fraser and Butler, and recent recognition-theoretic AI ethics, as a distinctively infrastructural form: misrecognition carried not by attitudes or cultural patterns but by the architecture through which recognition is delivered. That is also why I read Aadhaar not as an outlier but as an early instance of a form towards which algorithmic governance is converging. The obligations it owes are the obligations every such system will owe.
