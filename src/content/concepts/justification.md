---
title: Justification
definition: What makes a belief reasonable to hold — evidence, reliable formation, or the exercise of an intellectual virtue.
order: 11
relations:
  - to: knowledge
    type: grounds
    note: On the traditional analysis, justification is what turns true belief into knowledge.
  - to: explanation
    type: requires
    note: Justifications can be given; a belief that could not be defended by anyone is hard to call justified.
  - to: trust
    type: extends
    note: "Much of our justification is borrowed: we are justified because those we trust are."
areas: [epistemology]
---

Internalists say justification lives in reasons the believer can access; externalists say it lives in the reliability of the process. Machine outputs are a strange case for both: the process may be reliable, but no one — not the user, not the developer, not the system — can access the reasons.
