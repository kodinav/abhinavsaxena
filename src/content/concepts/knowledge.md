---
title: Knowledge
definition: Belief that is true and held for the right kind of reason — though what "the right kind" means has never been settled.
order: 4
spine: true
relations:
  - to: justification
    type: requires
    note: Knowledge is not lucky truth. Something must make the belief non-accidental.
  - to: testimony
    type: mediates
    note: Almost everything we know we know from others. Machine outputs are now among the others.
  - to: understanding
    type: extends
    note: Understanding is knowledge that grasps why; it is what a system may lack even when its outputs are true.
  - to: ethics
    type: grounds
    note: What we owe each other epistemically — honesty, care, the sharing of evidence — is where knowledge becomes ethical.
  - to: trust
    type: requires
    note: Knowing through others is only possible if trust can be placed well.
areas: [epistemology, social-epistemology]
---

The classical analysis of knowledge assumed a knower: a subject who believes, who has reasons, who can be asked to defend them. Machine epistemology puts pressure on each of those assumptions in turn. Can a model believe? Can it have reasons rather than merely produce them? Can it be asked?
