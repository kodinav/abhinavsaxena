Misrecognition is the failure to accord someone the standing, regard or status due to them, understood in recognition theory as a distinct kind of wrong rather than a by-product of maldistribution. The tradition descends from Hegel. Honneth locates recognition in three spheres: love secures self-confidence, legal recognition self-respect, and social esteem self-worth, and misrecognition in each is a distinct moral injury and a spur to struggle. Fraser recasts it as a matter of status: misrecognition is institutionalised subordination, the denial of standing to participate as a peer, and its remedy is parity of participation. Butler adds that recognisability is prior to recognition, since a subject's legibility is conditioned by norms she did not author. Within AI ethics, Waelen has proposed recognition as a guiding normative concept.

My work extends this programme to a form of misrecognition the canonical theories did not have in view. When a labourer's worn fingerprint fails to authenticate at the ration shop, nothing expresses cultural devaluation and no one withholds esteem. It is an architectural non-reading. The system's criterion of subjecthood, a readable fingerprint, is not neutral between bodies: it renders illegible those marked by the labour the welfare system exists to protect. In Fraser's terms this is a status-recognition failure, but it is effected through the design of an identification architecture rather than through patterns of cultural value. I call it infrastructural misrecognition, and the condition it reveals [constitutive dependence](/glossary/constitutive-dependence/).

Arendt's stateless person lost the right to have rights by being cast outside every political community. The subject of constitutive dependence is inside a polity that acknowledges her in law, yet its operative infrastructure withholds the recognition through which that legal standing is made effective. The wrong is a gap, opened and administered by the institution itself, between holding a right and being recognised as its bearer where it must be exercised.

Recognition theory is diagnostic. It tells us the labourer is wronged when the system fails to read her. It does not say what a particular institution owes in virtue of producing recognition, which is the gap the [Constitutive Obligation Principle](/glossary/constitutive-obligation-principle/) fills.
