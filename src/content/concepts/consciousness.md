---
title: Consciousness
definition: The fact that there is something it is like to be a given creature — experience from the inside, as opposed to function seen from the outside.
order: 8
relations:
  - to: mind
    type: grounds
    note: Consciousness is what makes mind more than information processing, if it is more.
  - to: agency
    type: challenges
    note: If action for reasons does not require experience, then agency and consciousness can come apart — as they may in machines.
  - to: ethics
    type: grounds
    note: Moral status has traditionally tracked the capacity for experience; the question of machine consciousness is therefore a moral question.
areas: [philosophy-of-mind, phenomenology]
---

I take the hard problem seriously and take seriously that it may not be solvable by philosophy alone. What philosophy can do is get clear about what would and would not count as evidence, and resist the two easy answers: that machines obviously are conscious because they say so, and that they obviously are not because they are machines.
